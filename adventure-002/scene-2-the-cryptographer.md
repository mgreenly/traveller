## Introduction Section

### Finding Binary Tessa
**Primary Discovery Method:** Ship service bill includes her flyer

Players might learn about Tessa through:
- **Computers (TN 8) on crypto forums:** Find "BinaryT" with stellar reviews for military-grade encryption
- **Streetwise (TN 9) asking about encryption:** "Only Binary Tessa can handle Q7 encryption without triggering killswitches"
- **Investigate (TN 10) at starport tech shops:** "Three people on-planet can crack military crypto. Two work for corps, one freelances."
- **Automatic:** Ship service includes flyer: "Data Resurrection Services - Military Grade Encryption Specialist"

### What Others Say
- **Tech vendor:** "Tessa? Brilliant but bitter. Imperial Navy kicked her out for something she didn't do. She's got a grudge against authority but she's the best there is."
- **Competitor:** "She's got some kind of backdoor into military systems. Don't know how, don't ask. Just know she can crack anything."
- **Previous client:** "Fair warning - she makes copies of interesting data. Says it's for 'insurance.' Never seen her sell anyone out though."

**Location:** Tech District, Level -3, Shop 47B, "Data Resurrection Services"

> **Read Aloud:** The tech district lies three levels below K'Kirka's main concourse, where the air smells of hot circuitry and ozone. Holographic signs advertise everything from neural implants to quantum processors. "Data Resurrection Services" occupies a narrow storefront squeezed between a cybernetics clinic and a robot repair shop. Through the grimy transparisteel window, you see walls lined with quantum servers, their cooling systems creating an endless white noise. A woman in her thirties with silvered neural interface ports behind each ear hunches over multiple holographic displays, lines of code reflecting in her augmented eyes.

## Meeting Binary Tessa

### Initial Approach
**Reaction Roll:** 2D6 + highest SOC modifier
- 2-5: Suspicious and dismissive ("Shop's closed. Come back never.")
- 6-8: Cautiously interested ("Depends what you need and what you're paying.")  
- 9-11: Professional welcome ("Data recovery, encryption cracking, or forensic reconstruction?")
- 12+: Eager for challenge ("Please tell me it's something interesting for once.")

### Negotiation Checks

#### Stage 1: Explaining the Job (Choose approach)

**Persuade (TN 8):** Emphasize the technical challenge and uniqueness
- Success: Tessa's eyes light up. "Quantum encryption with corruption patterns? Now that's interesting." (+2 to Stage 2)
- Failure: "Sounds like military-grade trouble. Price just doubled."

**Deception (TN 9):** Claim it's routine corporate data recovery
- Success: She seems to buy it initially (+1 to Stage 2)
- Failure: "Nice try. I can smell Imperial Intelligence encryption from here." (-2 to Stage 2, price increases)

**Computers (TN 7):** Speak her language about the technical specifications
- Success: "Finally, someone who knows what they're talking about." (+3 to Stage 2)
- Failure: She corrects your terminology dismissively (-1 to Stage 2)

#### Stage 2: Negotiating Terms

**Base Price:** Cr25,000 for standard encryption sections, Cr50,000 for corrupted data reconstruction

**Broker (TN 10 + modifiers from Stage 1):**
- Failure: Full price plus Cr10,000 "hazard fee"
- Success: Standard price
- Effect 4+: 20% discount and she throws in some decryption software

**Alternatives to Payment:**
- Share specific data findings (she keeps copies)
- Trade for military-grade encryption software you might have
- Promise of future jobs (requires successful Persuade TN 9)
- Intimidation (automatic failure - she has underworld protection)

### The Work Session

If hired, Tessa needs 2-3 days with access to the data core. During this time:

#### Day 1: Initial Analysis
> **Read Aloud:** Tessa's fingers dance across haptic interfaces as streams of data cascade across her screens. "Your Dr. Aros was paranoid. Good for him. These encryption layers... wait." She freezes. "This signature. I've seen it before. Two months ago, someone hired me to crack a similar pattern. Called themselves 'Sparrow.' Small galaxy, eh?"

**Player Decision:** Share information about Sparrow or keep quiet?
- If shared: Tessa provides Sparrow's old contact protocols (might still work)
- If hidden: Persuade (TN 8) to convince her you don't know anything

#### Day 2: Breakthrough
**Electronics check by Tessa (rolls 2D6+4):**
- 10+: Cracks the Kane Contingency and one additional file
- 12+: Also partially reconstructs corrupted Project Prometheus data
- 14+: Discovers hidden partition with coordinates to a secret Makhidkarun (mah-KHID-kah-run) black site

#### Day 3: Complications (Roll 2D6)

| Roll | Complication |
|------|--------------|
| 2-3  | Tessa's shop is raided by "building inspectors" (corporate agents) |
| 4-5  | A virus in the data core attacks her systems, she demands Cr10,000 for damages |
| 6-8  | Smooth work, no complications |
| 9-10 | She finds something that scares her: "This needs to go to the authorities" |
| 11-12| Another client offers her Cr100,000 for copies of your data |

### Tessa's Secrets

**Background (Learn with Investigate TN 10 or Carouse TN 8):**
- Ex-Imperial Navy cryptographer, dishonorably discharged
- Caught selling classified codes (actually framed by superior officer)
- Has backdoors into several Imperial Navy databases
- Secretly searching for evidence to clear her name

**Hidden Agenda:**
If she finds anything about Section 86 or Imperial Intelligence in the files, she copies it without telling the players (Notice TN 12 to spot this).

### Useful Information Tessa Can Provide

1. **About the Encryption:** "This isn't standard Makhidkarun (mah-KHID-kah-run). Someone modified their protocols. Whoever encrypted this had insider knowledge but wanted to hide it FROM Makhidkarun (mah-KHID-kah-run)."

2. **About the Hardware:** "You'll need molecular tools for those hidden partitions. Try Khalid over at Precision Instruments, but don't mention my name. We have... history."

3. **About the Corruption:** "This wasn't accidental damage. Someone tried to delete specific sections with a targeted virus. Sloppy work though - I can recover about 70%."

4. **Optional Side Job:** "Since you're obviously not afraid of dangerous data, I have a rush job. Cr15,000 to decrypt a competitor's client list. Interested?"

5. **About Cthonia Data Recovery:** "That radiation damage pattern? I can write a custom reconstruction algorithm. It'll cost you Cr10,000, but I guarantee 90% recovery. The Scout Service uses similar software but theirs is locked down tight."

### Ongoing Relationship

If the job goes well, Tessa becomes a contact who can:
- Provide cybersecurity for the ship (Cr5,000/month)
- Decrypt future finds (at discount rates)
- Share underworld information about data crimes
- Warn when someone's searching for the players in databases
