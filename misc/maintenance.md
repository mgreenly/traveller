# Starship Maintenance Guide

**MAINTENANCE** = Preventative upkeep to prevent failures (costs 0.1% per year)

Maintenance prevents system failures and must be paid every Maintenance Period (4 weeks). There are two mutually exclusive approaches - choose one or the other, never both.

---

## Maintenance Types

### Annual Maintenance
**What**: Pay 0.1% of ship value once per year at a starport  
**Where**: Class A or B starport with shipyard facilities  
**When**: Once per year  
**Duration**: 2 weeks downtime  

**Requirements:**
- **Starport**: Class A or B with shipyard
- **No crew involvement**: Professional technicians do all work
- **No tools needed**: Starport provides everything
- **Ship offline**: All systems shut down for maintenance

**Advantages:**
- Professional service with high-TL parts
- Complete system access (ship powered down)
- No crew skill requirements
- Comprehensive inspection

**Disadvantages:**
- 2 weeks complete downtime
- Large lump sum payment (0.1% of ship value)
- Must reach Class A/B starport
- Cannot address issues as they arise

### Ongoing Maintenance
**What**: Pay 0.1% ÷ 12 monthly while crew does the work  
**Where**: Aboard ship using ship's facilities  
**When**: Continuous throughout the year  
**Duration**: Small amounts of time regularly  

**Requirements:**
- **Engineer**: Must have qualified engineer on crew
- **Tools**: Standard starship engineer tool set per engineer
- **Workshop**: Highly recommended (allows making parts)
- **Spare Parts**: Must maintain inventory for monthly work
- **Safety Procedures**: Lockout-tagout protocols

**Engineer Tool Set Required (per engineer):**
- Multispectral vision goggles
- Iris valve opener
- Electronic and gravitic testing equipment
- Soldering gear and micromanipulators
- Pliers, wrenches, sockets, mallet
- Hull patches and portable airlock

**Scheduling Maintenance Work:**
- **In Jump Space**: Work on manoeuvre drive
- **In Normal Space**: Work on jump drive
- **Power Plant**: Work on individual reactors while others run
- **Life Support**: Work during low-demand periods

**Advantages:**
- Minimal downtime
- Problems caught early
- Flexible scheduling
- Monthly payment spreading
- Engineer gains intimate knowledge of systems

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires skilled crew
- Ship must carry tools and parts
- Safety risks (working on live systems)
- Cannot handle all high-TL components

## What Happens If Maintenance Is Skipped

When you skip maintenance payments, systems start failing. This applies to both methods - if you choose annual maintenance but don't go to a starport within the year, or if you choose ongoing maintenance but don't pay monthly costs.

### Failure Mechanics
1. **Skip a Maintenance Period**: Note how many periods skipped
2. **Every subsequent period**: Roll 2D with DM = periods skipped
3. **Result 8+**: Ship suffers a critical hit (system failure)
4. **Roll on Poor Maintenance Table**: Determine what broke

### Poor Maintenance Critical Hits (2D)
| Roll | System | Effect |
|------|--------|--------|
| 2-4 | Fuel Leak | Lose 1D × 10% fuel capacity |
| 5-7 | Drive Damaged | Roll 1D: M-Drive (1-3) or J-Drive (4-6) failure |
| 8-9 | Weapon Faulty | Random weapon gets DM-1 to attacks |
| 10-12 | Power Plant | 25% power loss, radiation exposure (2D×10 rads/week) |

## Workshop Benefits

Ships equipped with workshops gain significant advantages for ongoing maintenance:
- **Manufacture parts** from raw materials
- **Reduce spare parts** inventory requirements
- **Perform complex maintenance** tasks
- **Increase efficiency** of maintenance work

**Workshop Requirements:**
- Dedicated workspace
- Manufacturing equipment
- Raw material storage
- Qualified technician

## Safety Procedures

### Lockout-Tagout Protocols
- **Physical locks** on all systems under maintenance
- **Software locks** considered inadequate
- **Complete system shutdown** preferred for safety
- **Annual maintenance**: Entire ship locked out
- **Ongoing maintenance**: Individual systems locked out as needed

### Risk Management
Working on live ship systems carries inherent dangers:
- **Reactor exposure**: Radiation hazards
- **Pressure systems**: Explosive decompression
- **Gravitic fields**: Equipment damage/injury
- **Electrical systems**: Shock/electrocution

*"Safety procedures may seem like unnecessary formalities, but they should usually be complied with as most are written in the blood of dead crew."*

## Annual Maintenance Scheduling

### Planning Considerations
- **Schedule starport berth** well in advance
- **Coordinate with crew leave** schedules
- **Consider seasonal trade** patterns
- **Allow buffer time** for unexpected issues

### Pre-Maintenance Checklist
- Prepare complete systems documentation
- Compile list of known issues
- Arrange temporary crew accommodations
- Plan cargo handling if needed

### Post-Maintenance Verification
- Complete systems check
- Test all critical functions
- Update maintenance logs
- Verify parts inventory
- Review any outstanding issues

---

## Regulatory Compliance

### Imperial Requirements
- **Annual inspections** for commercial vessels
- **Maintenance records** must be kept
- **Safety equipment** must be certified
- **Hazardous materials** properly documented

### Insurance Considerations
- **Maintained logs** may reduce premiums
- **Skipped maintenance** may void coverage
- **Professional maintenance** preferred by insurers
- **Document all work** and replacements
