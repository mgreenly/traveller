## Chapter Summaries

### Introduction (Page 3)
Overview of the Traveller Companion as a comprehensive toolkit for customizing campaigns. Emphasizes the optional nature of all rules and the ability to pick and choose elements that fit specific play styles. Explains the philosophy of providing alternatives to core rulebook concepts and support for different campaign tones from realistic to heroic to narrative.

### Characteristics (Pages 4-12)
Extended characteristic systems including rules for characteristics above 15, additional characteristics like Wealth (WLT), Luck, Reputation, and other abstract attributes. Provides alternative ways to handle character capabilities beyond the standard six physical and mental characteristics, allowing for more detailed character differentiation and specialized campaign needs.

### Alternate Traveller Creation (Pages 13-24)
Multiple alternative character generation systems providing different levels of player control over the creation process. Includes point-buy systems, guided creation methods, and specialized generation rules for different campaign types. Allows referees to tailor character creation to match desired power levels and thematic requirements.

### Allies, Contacts, Rivals and Enemies (Pages 25-31)
Expanded relationship systems providing detailed mechanics for generating and managing the network of NPCs that surround each character. Includes methods for determining relationship strength, favors owed, and how these relationships evolve during play. Essential for campaigns emphasizing social interaction and political maneuvering.

### Pre-Career Options (Pages 32-34)
Additional background generation options that occur before formal career selection. Covers childhood experiences, education variations, and formative events that shape characters before they enter their first career. Provides more detailed backstory generation for character-driven campaigns.

### Additional Careers (Pages 35-40)
New career paths beyond those in the core rulebook, providing options for specialized character concepts and different setting requirements. Includes unique career progression tables, benefits, and skills that expand character creation possibilities for diverse campaign needs.

### Training and Experience (Pages 41-42)
Alternative systems for skill development during play, allowing characters to grow and learn beyond simple advancement rules. Provides mechanics for formal training, learning from experience, and skill development that reflects character activities during adventures.

### Broad Skills and Specialities (Pages 43-46)
Alternative skill systems that group related abilities under broader categories or allow for more detailed specialization within skills. Provides flexibility for campaigns that want either simplified skill resolution or highly detailed expertise systems.

### Alternate Play Styles (Pages 47-48)
Rules for narrative task resolution, dramatic success/failure mechanics, and other alternatives to traditional dice-based resolution. Includes systems for more cinematic or story-focused campaigns that prioritize narrative outcomes over detailed simulation.

### Combat (Pages 49-57)
Extended combat rules including hit location systems, wound severity mechanics, alternate initiative systems, and expanded tactical options. Provides tools for making combat more detailed and tactical or streamlined depending on campaign preferences.

### Gravity and Related Effects (Pages 58-63)
Comprehensive rules for gravitational variations and their effects on characters, equipment, and activities. Covers high-gravity and low-gravity adaptations, gravitational injuries, and the long-term effects of living in non-standard gravity environments.

### Atmosphere and Vacuum (Pages 64-70)
Detailed atmospheric hazard rules covering toxic atmospheres, pressure variations, corrosive environments, and vacuum exposure. Provides realistic consequences for environmental dangers and tools for creating challenging atmospheric conditions.

### Diseases, Toxins and Chemicals (Pages 71-79)
Comprehensive biological and chemical hazard systems covering diseases, poisons, drugs, and environmental toxins. Includes creation systems for custom hazards and detailed rules for infection, treatment, and long-term health consequences.

### Starvation and Thirst (Pages 80-81)
Survival mechanics covering food and water deprivation, their effects on character performance, and recovery procedures. Essential for wilderness survival campaigns and situations where basic resources are limited.

### Effects of Temperature (Pages 82-83)
Environmental temperature rules covering extreme heat and cold, their physiological effects, and protective measures. Includes hypothermia, heat stroke, and equipment failure due to temperature extremes.

### Terrain, Conditions and Movement (Pages 84-92)
Comprehensive movement rules covering different terrain types, weather conditions, and environmental obstacles. Provides detailed mechanics for navigation, movement speeds, and the challenges of traveling in hostile environments.

### Animal Encounters (Pages 93-110)
Extensive creature interaction system covering animal behavior, encounter procedures, and detailed rules for hunting, taming, and dealing with dangerous wildlife. Includes comprehensive tables for generating animal encounters and determining creature responses.

### Vehicle Damage (Page 111)
Extended rules for vehicle combat damage, system failures, and repair procedures. Provides more detailed consequences for vehicle combat beyond simple hit point loss.

### Refereeing Traveller (Pages 112-119)
Advanced referee guidance covering campaign management, adventure design, NPC creation, and handling complex situations. Provides tools and advice for running more sophisticated and engaging campaigns.

### Interpreting UWP Data (Pages 120-124)
Guide to analyzing and expanding Universal World Profile data to create rich, detailed worlds. Covers how to interpret the statistical data and transform it into narrative descriptions and adventure opportunities.

### Travellermap and the Wiki (Pages 125-132)
Integration guide for using online Traveller resources including Travellermap.com and the Traveller Wiki. Provides tools for incorporating community-generated content and accessing the vast database of Traveller universe information.

### Starports and Spaceports (Pages 133-142)
Extended starport classification system with additional port types, services, and complications. Covers specialized facilities, hidden ports, and the economic and political factors that shape starport operations.

### Legalities and Lawbreaking (Pages 143-147)
Comprehensive legal system covering crime, punishment, legal procedures, and the consequences of lawbreaking. Includes rules for different legal systems, corruption, and the complexities of interstellar law enforcement.

### Slower-Than-Light (Page 148)
Rules for campaigns or situations involving travel at sublight speeds. Covers generation ships, relativistic effects, and the unique challenges of STL interstellar travel.

### The Jump Drive (Pages 149-155)
Extended jump travel mechanics covering misjumps, jump space encounters, drive failures, and the hazards of interstellar travel. Provides tools for making jump travel more dangerous and unpredictable.

### Travelling in Normal Space (Pages 156-159)
Detailed space travel rules for movement within star systems. Covers orbital mechanics, acceleration limits, fuel consumption, and the realities of space navigation and maneuvering.

### Mine Warfare (Pages 160-162)
Space mine combat systems including mine types, deployment procedures, detection methods, and countermeasures. Adds tactical complexity to space combat and provides tools for space-based defensive scenarios.

### Gas Giant Operations (Pages 163-164)
Procedures for fuel skimming and operations in gas giant atmospheres. Covers the hazards, equipment requirements, and tactical considerations of atmospheric refueling operations.

### Transponders, Registry and Mortgages (Pages 165-166)
Ship identification systems, ownership documentation, and the legal framework surrounding starship ownership. Covers ship registration, mortgages, and the bureaucratic aspects of ship ownership.

### Missiles and Torpedoes (Pages 167-168)
Extended missile combat rules covering different warhead types, guidance systems, countermeasures, and tactical missile deployment. Adds depth and variety to space combat encounters.

### Vector-Based Space Combat (Pages 169-177)
Alternative space combat system emphasizing realistic space physics, vector movement, and tactical positioning. Provides a more simulation-focused approach to space combat for groups preferring realistic space warfare.

### Starship Automation (Page 178)
Rules for automated ship systems, computer-controlled functions, and the advantages and vulnerabilities of ship automation. Covers both helpful automation and potential system failures.

### Gravitic Shielding (Page 179)
Advanced defensive technology systems providing protection against various forms of attack. Covers how gravitic shields interact with weapons and the tactical implications of this technology.

### Starship Weaponry (Pages 180-182)
Extended ship weapon systems beyond those in the core rules. Covers specialized weapons, weapon modifications, and advanced targeting systems that add variety to ship design and combat.

## Quick Reference

**Purpose**: Comprehensive toolkit for customizing and enhancing Traveller campaigns

**Usage**: Optional rules to be selected based on campaign needs and referee preferences

**Key Features**:
- Alternative character generation systems
- Extended environmental and survival rules
- Advanced combat and space travel mechanics
- Referee tools and campaign enhancement options
- Integration with online Traveller resources

**Campaign Types**: Supports realistic hard science-fiction, narrative/cinematic play, survival-focused adventures, detailed tactical combat, and specialized setting requirements

**Integration**: All rules designed to work with core Traveller mechanics while providing optional enhancements and alternatives

**Essential Information**: Character creation alternatives (13-24), environmental hazards (58-92), space travel enhancements (149-177), referee tools (112-132)
