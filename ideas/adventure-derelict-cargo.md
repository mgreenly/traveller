# Derelict Cargo Parasites
*Survival Horror Adventure Concept*

## Core Concept

The *Weyland's Pride*, a bulk cargo hauler operating the lonely route between the frontier mining worlds and the core systems, responds to an automated distress beacon from what appears to be a research station on LV-426-Echo, a moon that shouldn't have any installations according to official records. The crew, motivated by salvage rights that could set them up for life, discovers Research Station Prometheus, a black-site xenobiology facility that's been dark for three years. Inside, they find evidence of experiments with parasitic organisms designed as biological weapons—creatures that reproduce through living hosts, adapt to any environment, and exhibit a terrifying combination of cunning intelligence and perfect killing instinct.

This Alien-inspired adventure transforms the player characters from explorers seeking profit into desperate survivors trying to prevent an extinction-level threat from reaching populated space. The parasites aren't just dangerous—they're perfectly evolved biological weapons that use human bodies as incubators, human ships as transportation, and human nature as their greatest asset. Every decision carries weight: save a potentially infected crew member and risk spreading the contamination, or abandon them and live with the moral consequences.

## Setting and Atmosphere

The *Weyland's Pride* is a working-class vessel, two hundred meters of functional ugliness designed to haul ore and processed materials across the vast emptiness between systems. Twenty years of constant use shows in every rivet and weld. The crew quarters are cramped, the mess hall doubles as the medical bay, and privacy is a luxury no one can afford. The cargo holds, vast caverns of metal that echo with every footstep, take up three-quarters of the ship's volume. The vessel's age means constant maintenance—something's always breaking, life support runs at 80% efficiency on a good day, and the lighting flickers ominously even under normal conditions.

Research Station Prometheus tells a different story. Built into the moon's crust, it's a state-of-the-art facility that clearly had unlimited funding. Multiple laboratory levels descend into the rock, each one more secured than the last. Observation chambers with two-foot-thick transparent aluminum windows. Decontamination airlocks between every section. An automated security system that should have prevented any breach. But all the precautions failed. The blast doors are torn open from the inside. Brown stains that might be old blood streak the walls. And everywhere, there's a organic resin coating the walls—a substance that seems to pulse with its own life.

The moon itself is hostile to human life. The atmosphere is toxic, requiring environment suits for any exterior movement. The surface is covered in a fine, corrosive dust that eats through seals and filters. Underground, the research station extends through natural cavern systems that predate the facility, caverns that show signs of being shaped rather than formed, though by what remains unclear. The deeper levels of the station connect to these caverns, and something has been building down there—structures that serve no human purpose, architected by an intelligence that thinks in terms of biological efficiency rather than comfort.

## The Cast of Characters

**Captain Marcus Webb** has run the *Weyland's Pride* for fifteen years, keeping her flying through determination and creative interpretations of safety regulations. He's seen enough to know that corporate research stations don't go dark without good reason, but the potential salvage could mean retirement for his entire crew. His protective instinct toward his crew conflicts with his pragmatic understanding that sometimes people have to be sacrificed for the greater good.

**Dr. Elena Vasquez**, the ship's medic and only person with scientific training, immediately recognizes the research station's true purpose from the equipment present. She understands the implications of what was being developed here—biological weapons that violate every treaty in civilized space. Her medical oath to do no harm conflicts with the necessity of potentially killing infected crew members before they can become incubators.

**Engineering Chief Roy Bates** keeps the *Weyland's Pride* running with spit, wire, and profanity. He's the first to notice the organic resin can conduct electricity and seems to integrate with the station's systems. His practical knowledge becomes invaluable when conventional solutions fail, but his tendency to touch things he shouldn't makes him a prime candidate for infection.

**Navigation Officer Kim Park** discovers the station's logs were deliberately purged, but manages to recover fragments that hint at the parasite's origin—not created but discovered, found in the ancient caverns beneath the station. She becomes obsessed with understanding what happened, pursuing knowledge that might save them or damn them all.

**Cargo Specialist Dutch Mueller** knows every inch of the ship's massive holds, which becomes crucial when they need to quarantine potentially infected areas. His discovery of similar organic resin beginning to appear in the ship's ventilation system suggests the contamination has already spread beyond the station.

**Corporate Observer Ms. White** joined the crew at the last port, claiming to be conducting routine efficiency evaluations. In reality, she works for the same company that ran the research station and has orders to retrieve samples at any cost. Her hidden agenda and willingness to sacrifice the crew for the organism makes her as dangerous as the parasites themselves.

## The Parasite Lifecycle

The organisms follow a horrifying reproductive cycle that weaponizes human biology. The initial stage involves microscopic spores that can survive in vacuum and extreme temperatures. Once inhaled or absorbed through skin contact, they migrate to the chest cavity and begin growing. The host experiences mild flu-like symptoms for 24-48 hours while the parasite gestates, during which they remain mobile and unaware of their condition.

The emergence is violent and invariably fatal. The mature parasite, already the size of a small dog, bursts from the host's chest cavity in a shower of blood and bone. Within hours, it grows to full size—a biomechanical nightmare of armor plating, acidic blood, and retractable inner jaws capable of punching through steel. But it's the intelligence that makes them truly terrifying. They learn, adapt, and hunt with patience and cunning. They understand technology enough to disable systems, set traps, and even use the station's infrastructure against intruders.

Most disturbing is their method of creating new hosts. Mature parasites can implant embryos directly through a grotesque proboscis, or they can create egg sacs in warm, humid environments. The research station's lower levels have been transformed into a hive, walls covered in the same organic resin that hardens into a structure both architectural and biological. Humans caught and brought here don't die immediately—they're preserved in the resin, kept alive as long as possible to serve as incubators for multiple generations of parasites.

## Escalating Terror

The adventure begins with exploration and salvage, the crew excited about their find. The first signs are subtle—scratches on the walls that look like claw marks, rooms that have been sealed from the inside, personal effects abandoned in hallways as if people fled in panic. The station's logs, what little remain, mention a containment breach but not its resolution.

The first direct encounter might be with a long-dead parasite, giving the crew false confidence that whatever happened is over. But then someone notices the egg sacs in the lower levels, some of them recently opened. A crew member complains of feeling sick but insists it's just the recycled air. Strange noises echo through the station's ventilation system—sounds that might be settling metal or might be something moving with purpose.

When the first crew member is infected, the moral dilemmas begin. They seem fine, insisting they just need rest, but the medical scans show something growing inside them. Do you quarantine them? Attempt surgery? Or make the hard choice while you still can? The situation deteriorates rapidly once the first parasite emerges. Now it's hunting, and the station's layout that seemed merely inconvenient becomes a death trap of blind corners and sealed bulkheads.

## The Hive Discovery

The true horror reveals itself in the station's depths. The research team didn't create these organisms—they found them in the pre-existing caverns, preserved in some form of natural stasis for millions of years. The scientists thought they could study and weaponize them, but they underestimated the parasites' intelligence and adaptability. The deeper caverns have been transformed into a massive hive structure, with hundreds of egg sacs and dozens of human hosts in various stages of incubation.

The hive's queen—larger, smarter, and more dangerous than the drones—has been laying eggs continuously for three years. She's learned from the research station's attempts to contain her offspring, adapting her children to resist fire, survive in vacuum, and even mimic human behavior to a limited degree. The organic resin isn't just structural—it's a communication network, allowing the parasites to coordinate across the entire complex.

Most terrifying is the discovery that one ship already left the station after the breach—the research vessel *Darwin's Folly*, headed for a populated system with a full complement of infected crew. The players must decide whether to pursue and stop it, risking their own infection, or warn authorities and hope they're believed in time.

## Survival and Escape

Fighting the parasites directly is nearly suicidal. Their acidic blood makes close combat deadly to the attacker. They're faster, stronger, and more resilient than humans. Fire works but risks damaging life support systems. The vacuum of space kills them eventually, but they can survive long enough to breach hull plating. The only real defense is prevention—maintaining quarantine protocols, using motion sensors to track movement, and never going anywhere alone.

The *Weyland's Pride* becomes a fortress under siege. Every system must be checked for contamination. Every crew member must be monitored for infection. The cargo holds, once empty spaces, become killing fields where parasites could be lurking. The ventilation system, necessary for life, becomes a highway for creatures that can compress themselves through impossibly small spaces.

Escape requires terrible choices. The station must be destroyed to prevent further spread, but the nuclear self-destruct has been disabled. Someone must manually overload the reactor. The *Weyland's Pride* might already be contaminated, meaning reaching populated space could doom billions. The corporate observer's revelation that she has a faster ship hidden nearby offers escape, but at what cost?

## The Final Choice

The adventure climaxes with an impossible decision. The players have learned the parasites aren't just random predators—they're a form of biological terraforming, designed by some ancient intelligence to repurpose biological matter into more of themselves. Every infected world becomes a hive. Every species becomes raw material. They're not just facing a dangerous organism; they're facing an extinction event.

The *Darwin's Folly* is still in communication range, its crew unaware they're infected. Warning them means revealing the players' location to the corporate observer's superiors, who will prioritize retrieving samples over containing the outbreak. Not warning them means an inhabited system of three billion souls faces infection. And somewhere in the hive, the queen has learned enough about human technology to begin modifying her offspring to survive jump space, meaning nowhere in the galaxy would be safe.

The final escape, if it comes, carries no triumph. Too many have died. The survivors carry both physical and psychological scars. And everyone knows it's not over—the parasites are patient, adaptable, and somewhere out in the black, more eggs wait in forgotten corners of space, preserved for millions of years, waiting for the next curious crew to discover them and begin the cycle anew.
