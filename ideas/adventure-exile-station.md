# Exile Station
*Inspired by The Great Escape/Escape from Alcatraz but in a asteroid prison*

## Concept
The crew gets deliberately arrested and sent to Exile Station, a maximum-security prison built into a hollowed-out asteroid in the Sword Worlds, to extract a political prisoner who has vital intelligence about an imminent Sword Worlds attack on Imperial shipping lanes. The prison is considered escape-proof because it's in a decaying orbit around a gas giant with a 47-day window before it plunges into the atmosphere.

## Key Elements
- The prison economy runs on reputation and favors, not credits
- Multiple factions control different sections: Imperial prisoners, Sword Worlders, Vargr packs, and "neutrals"
- The warden knows exactly why the crew is there and is playing their own game
- Guards are mostly automated systems with a small human oversight team
- Previous escape attempts have left behind infrastructure the crew can exploit

## Memorable NPCs
- **Warden Elisabeth Krueger**: Former Imperial Intelligence, running the prison as her personal fiefdom
- **"Professor" Hadrian Vale**: The target, a genius engineer who's gone slightly mad in isolation
- **Big Tom Sullivan**: Lifer who runs the Imperial section, suspicious but potentially helpful
- **Ghazak-Ko**: Vargr pack leader who controls the maintenance tunnels
- **Serial Number 4471**: A prisoner who's actually an undercover journalist gathering material

## Prison Sections
- **The Pit**: General population, carved from asteroid rock, constant background radiation
- **The Greenhouse**: Food production area, heavily monitored but has real soil
- **The Factory**: Prisoners manufacture components for the Sword Worlds, access to tools
- **The Ice Mines**: Water extraction from frozen deposits, extremely dangerous
- **Solitary**: Isolated cells with psychological pressure systems
- **The Observatory**: Former asteroid survey dome, now the warden's office

## Complications
- The target refuses to leave without his "children" (maintenance robots he's reprogrammed)
- A prison riot breaks out during the escape attempt
- The Imperial prisoner they're extracting is actually a double agent
- Another crew is there to kill the target, not rescue them
- The decaying orbit is accelerating due to a gas giant storm
- The prison's AI has achieved sentience and has its own escape plan

## Escape Vectors
- Hijack the monthly supply shuttle (heavily guarded, limited window)
- Repair an abandoned mining vessel in the forgotten sections
- Use the prison's own emergency evacuation pods (sabotaged by previous attempts)
- Transmit themselves out using a hidden Ancient device the professor found
- Trigger a "transfer" to another facility and escape during transport
- Convert the entire asteroid into a ship using the factory and ice mines

## Social Dynamics
- Earn respect through prison competitions (zero-g boxing, asteroid mining records)
- Navigate complex barter economy (information, contraband, protection)
- Balance between maintaining cover and achieving objectives
- Form unlikely alliances with other inmates planning escapes
- Deal with prison informants and trustee prisoners

## Potential Outcomes
- Successful escape triggers reform of the prison system (or its destruction)
- The warden helps them escape in exchange for a favor to be claimed later
- They escape but have to bring unexpected prisoners who become recurring NPCs
- The prison is actually a cover for illegal experiments they must expose
- They prevent the asteroid from falling into the gas giant, becoming heroes to inmates
- The intelligence was fake, but they discover a real threat during the escape

## Environmental Challenges
- Radiation zones that require special protection or timed movement
- Zero-g sections where prisoners have adapted better than guards
- Pressure loss in damaged sections used as punishment areas
- Extreme temperature variations between heated and unheated zones
- Communication blackouts during gas giant storms
