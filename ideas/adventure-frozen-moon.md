# The Frozen Moon Parasite
*Paranoia Horror Adventure Concept*

## Core Concept

Deep beneath the ice of Khione-7, a remote mining moon in an uncharted system, Stellarion Mining Corporation operates a geothermal extraction facility with a crew of forty souls. The operation has been profitable for three years, until deep core drilling at the five-kilometer mark breached an ancient cavity containing a derelict vessel of unknown origin. This discovery should have been the find of the century. Instead, it unleashed something that had been waiting in the frozen dark for millennia: a shapeshifting organism that can perfectly replicate any biological entity it consumes, down to memories, mannerisms, and even DNA.

Drawing inspiration from John Carpenter's The Thing, this adventure transforms the isolated mining station into a pressure cooker of paranoia where trust becomes the most valuable and dangerous commodity. The organism doesn't just kill—it replaces, creating perfect duplicates indistinguishable from the originals except for subtle behavioral tells that only careful observation might reveal. As the situation deteriorates, the player characters must navigate not just the physical threat of the creature, but the psychological horror of not knowing who among the survivors is still human.

## Setting and Atmosphere

Khione-7 is a frozen hell barely worth the minerals it yields. Surface temperatures hover at minus 180 degrees Celsius, with methane-ammonia storms that can last for weeks. The mining station, designation Erebus Deep, consists of interconnected habitat modules buried twenty meters beneath the ice sheet for protection against radiation and storms. The only connection to the surface is a single elevator shaft leading to the landing pad, which freezes solid between supply runs.

The station itself reflects three years of corporate cost-cutting and personnel turnover. Environmental systems run at bare minimum to save power, keeping internal temperatures at a "comfortable" 10 degrees Celsius. Ice forms on interior walls during night cycles. The lighting flickers constantly due to power fluctuations from the geothermal tap. Narrow corridors barely wide enough for two people create natural choke points, while the constant groaning of ice pressure against the hull provides an unsettling soundtrack. The recreation room's viewing window, meant to boost morale, shows only impenetrable darkness—a constant reminder of their isolation.

The discovered derelict, designated Object Sigma, lies in a natural cavern five kilometers down, accessible only through the mining shaft. The vessel's architecture defies human understanding: corridors that loop back on themselves, surfaces that seem to shift when not directly observed, and a biological component integrated into the ship's structure that suggests the builders merged organic and mechanical technologies. The organism was found in what appeared to be stasis pods, though whether it was crew, cargo, or prisoner remains unknown.

## The Cast of Characters

**Director Margaret Cross** runs Erebus Deep with military efficiency, having served twenty years in the Imperial Navy before taking corporate credits. She discovered Object Sigma but kept it secret from corporate, planning to claim salvage rights personally. Her decision to thaw the first specimen started this nightmare, though she maintains strict denial about her culpability. She insists on maintaining station discipline even as paranoia destroys unit cohesion.

**Dr. James Petrov**, the station's medical officer and de facto scientist, performed the initial examination of the organism. He was likely the first replaced, though when this occurred remains uncertain. The Petrov-thing maintains his personality perfectly but pushes for decisions that isolate individuals and create opportunities for assimilation. His medical authority makes him particularly dangerous as he can declare people "infected" and quarantine them.

**Chief Engineer Rosa Martinez** maintains the station's failing systems through determination and creative repairs. She noticed unusual power draws from sections that should be empty and discovered organic material growing in the ventilation systems. Her paranoid reputation from years of isolation actually becomes an asset—she trusts no one and has already begun creating improvised weapons and detection methods.

**Miner Team Leader Jake "Frost" Morrison** led the team that breached Object Sigma. Half his team never returned from that shift, officially listed as a cave-in casualty. He knows they found something else down there—geometric structures that couldn't be natural, symbols that hurt to look at, and a warmth that shouldn't exist five kilometers down. He drinks heavily to forget what he saw, making him unreliable but potentially holding crucial information.

**Communications Officer Lin Zhou** maintains the station's only link to civilization, though the next supply ship isn't due for six weeks. She's noticed irregularities in personnel behavior patterns through security footage but hasn't shared her suspicions, unsure who to trust. Her access to station-wide surveillance makes her valuable to both humans and the organism.

**Corporate Auditor Franklin Weiss** arrived on the last supply run to investigate discrepancies in mineral yields. His obsession with documenting everything could provide crucial evidence of who's been replaced and when. However, his outsider status and natural suspicion of the crew make him an obvious target for false accusations when paranoia peaks.

## The Infection Vector

The organism operates through biological assimilation at the cellular level. Initial contact appears benign—the creature seems dead, frozen, harmless. But at temperatures above minus 40 Celsius, it begins regenerating. The first stage involves microscopic infiltration, injecting cells that begin converting the host from within. The process takes 6-12 hours for complete replacement, during which the victim experiences fever, disorientation, and gaps in memory they can't explain.

Once conversion completes, the duplicate is perfect. It possesses all memories, skills, and personality traits of the original. Only subtle tells remain: a slight delay when recalling emotional memories, an unusual fascination with getting others alone, and a complete inability to digest certain proteins that causes them to avoid specific foods. The organism can also split itself, creating multiple infections simultaneously, though this weakens each individual form.

Most terrifying is the creature's learning capability. Each assimilation adds to its collective knowledge. It learns station protocols, security codes, and personal relationships. It understands human psychology well enough to manipulate paranoia, turning the crew against each other. It might even fake being discovered as an imitation to eliminate a particularly troublesome human under the guise of "protection."

## Escalating Horror

The adventure begins with subtle wrongness. Someone uses the wrong keycode but it still works. The sled dogs kept for emergency surface travel won't stop barking at certain personnel. Food supplies show signs of tampering. Then comes the first discovery—a body part that shouldn't exist, a duplicate of someone still alive, hidden in the waste processing system.

Trust erodes rapidly once the threat is understood. Standard blood tests prove inconclusive as the organism perfectly mimics human biology. Someone suggests a heat test—the thing reacts violently to flame—but this creates opportunities for false accusations and murder disguised as protection. The station's tight quarters mean privacy doesn't exist; people must sleep, shower, and eat while wondering if the person next to them is still human.

System failures compound the paranoia. The organism understands technology and begins sabotaging critical systems to create chaos. Heating fails in sections, driving survivors together. Communications mysteriously fail whenever someone tries to warn the outside. The shuttle's navigation computer develops corruption that would send it to a populated system instead of the designated quarantine coordinates.

## Detection and Combat

Fighting the organism requires improvisation and intelligence over firepower. Conventional weapons cause it to split into smaller, independent entities. Flame proves effective but risks destroying the station's already fragile life support. Extreme cold can force dormancy but doesn't kill it. The most effective solution might be trapping it and ejecting it into space, though this requires identifying all infected individuals first.

Detection methods evolve through trial and error. Blood samples exposed to heat cause violent reactions. Infected individuals avoid certain foods due to protein incompatibilities. They struggle with emotional responses to personal memories from before infection. Security footage, when carefully analyzed, shows behavioral inconsistencies. But each test carries the risk of false positives leading to murder of innocent humans.

## Endgame Scenarios

Multiple endings are possible depending on player actions and paranoia levels. If trust completely collapses, humans might kill each other while the organism escapes. Maintaining group cohesion might allow systematic testing to identify all infected, though the organism learns from each failed detection attempt. The nuclear self-destruct offers a final solution, but requires someone to stay behind.

The approaching supply ship creates a deadline. If not contained, the organism could reach populated space. Players might need to destroy the station, warn the ship, or attempt a desperate escape knowing some among them might be infected. The discovered derelict still holds secrets that might provide a cure or weapon against the organism, but returning to Object Sigma means facing whatever else waits in that ancient darkness.

Even successful escape carries no guarantee of safety. The organism is patient, clever, and possibly already among the survivors. The adventure's true horror lies not in the monster itself, but in how quickly civilization's veneer cracks when no one can be trusted. In the frozen dark of Khione-7, humanity's greatest enemy becomes itself.
