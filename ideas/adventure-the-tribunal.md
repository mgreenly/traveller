# The Tribunal
*Inspired by A Few Good Men/12 Angry Men but in Imperial court*

## Concept
The crew must defend their patron, Captain Marcus Reed, in an Imperial Navy tribunal where he's accused of treason for destroying a merchant vessel that was allegedly carrying refugees. The truth is more complex: the ship was carrying a biological weapon designed to look like a plague outbreak, and Reed made a command decision that saved millions but violated the rules of engagement. The crew must investigate, gather evidence, navigate Imperial legal procedures, and ultimately decide whether to reveal a conspiracy that goes to the highest levels of sector command.

## Key Elements
- Imperial Navy legal procedures are arcane, formal, and stacked against the defendant
- The trial takes place aboard INS Justice, a dedicated tribunal vessel in orbit around Regina
- Three-judge panel: one Navy, one civilian, one wild card (possibly alien or psionicist)
- The crew serves as Reed's legal advocates, investigators, and possibly as witnesses
- Evidence is being actively suppressed or destroyed by unknown parties
- Time limit: 10 days until the verdict, with formal sessions every other day
- Discovery of evidence between sessions through investigation, persuasion, and infiltration
- The refugee ship's "black box" data has been partially corrupted - deliberately or accidentally?

## Memorable NPCs

### The Accused
- **Captain Marcus Reed**: 20-year Navy veteran, by-the-book officer wrestling with his decision. Knows more than he initially reveals but is bound by oaths and classified orders. His family is being subtly threatened.

### The Prosecution
- **Commander Victoria Blackwood**: Lead prosecutor, ambitious and ruthless but not corrupt. Genuinely believes Reed is guilty but doesn't know about the bioweapon. Could become an ally if convinced of the truth.
- **Lt. Commander Yashi Tomoko**: Blackwood's assistant, secretly sympathetic to Reed but needs the crew to give her cover to help.

### The Judges
- **Admiral Constance Waverly**: Navy judge, old-school, values chain of command above all. Served with Reed's mentor.
- **Magistrate Kelvin Okonkwo**: Civilian judge, focused on legal precedent and protecting civilian lives. Lost family in a previous military "accident."
- **Arbiter Gvegh**: Vargr judge from the Gvegh-Aek, interested in pack loyalty vs. individual conscience.

### Witnesses & Related Parties
- **Dr. Sarah Chen**: Xenobiologist who examined debris from the destroyed ship, knows about the bioweapon but is terrified to testify.
- **"Ensign" Alex Morrison**: Fresh academy graduate who was on Reed's bridge, saw everything but is being pressured to change testimony.
- **Helena Cross**: War correspondent who has footage of the incident but wants exclusive rights to the crew's story.
- **Commander Jakob Stern**: Reed's former XO, now commands his own ship. Knows about similar incidents but speaking up would end his career.
- **The Ghost**: Anonymous source sending encrypted data to the crew, claims to be from Naval Intelligence.
- **Merchant Captain Tara Singh**: Survivor from another ship that responded to the "refugee" vessel's distress call, saw something that doesn't match official records.

## Investigation Phases

### Phase 1: Understanding the Charges
- Review official logs and sensor data (partially classified/redacted)
- Interview Reed to get his version of events
- Examine the prosecution's evidence list
- Discover discrepancies in the official timeline

### Phase 2: The Destroyed Ship
- Track down the "refugee" ship's origin and registration (leads to shell companies)
- Find surviving crew members in hiding (they were paid to deliver cargo, not told what)
- Analyze debris patterns that suggest internal explosion, not external weapons fire
- Discover medical reports from nearby systems about mysterious illness (the bioweapon's test runs)

### Phase 3: The Cover-Up
- Realize certain evidence is disappearing in real-time
- Identify who benefits from Reed's conviction (arms dealers, war hawks, foreign agents)
- Find the original, unedited sensor logs hidden in maintenance computer buffers
- Connect the bioweapon to a black project supposedly cancelled years ago

### Phase 4: The Conspiracy
- Trace orders through the chain of command to find who really gave the destroy order
- Discover a faction within the Navy preparing for war with the Zhodani
- Learn that Reed was supposed to let the ship through to create a crisis
- Find evidence of similar incidents being prepared in other sectors

## Courtroom Procedures

### Daily Trial Structure
1. **Morning Session**: Formal testimony and cross-examination
2. **Recess**: Time for investigation and witness preparation
3. **Afternoon Session**: Presentation of evidence and legal arguments
4. **Evening**: Private meetings, deal-making, and investigation

### Legal Mechanics
- **Advocacy Skill Checks**: Modified by evidence quality and witness credibility
- **Investigation Points**: Earned through roleplay and clever questioning
- **Reputation Track**: How the crew is perceived affects available options
- **Pressure Meter**: As the crew gets closer to the truth, opposition intensifies
- **Chain of Evidence**: Must maintain legal procedures or evidence is inadmissible

## Complications

### Early Trial
- Key witness dies in an "accident" the night before testifying
- The crew's ship is impounded on a technicality, limiting mobility
- Reed attempts suicide, leaving a note that implicates someone unexpected
- A junior member of the prosecution team seeks asylum with the crew

### Mid Trial
- The Zhodani Embassy offers to provide evidence in exchange for intelligence
- A terrorist attack on the tribunal ship forces a recess and changes security protocols
- The crew discovers one of the judges is being blackmailed
- Reed's family goes missing, and he refuses to cooperate until they're found

### Late Trial
- The real perpetrators offer the crew a deal: wealth and safety for silence
- Evidence surfaces that Reed has a secret past that undermines his credibility
- The Ghost is revealed to be someone the crew trusts
- A fleet emergency requires the trial to conclude immediately

### Variable Complications
- The bioweapon activates in the evidence locker, forcing quarantine
- A crew member is charged with contempt and imprisoned
- Protestors (both for and against Reed) complicate movement and meetings
- A media leak forces the trial into closed sessions

## Potential Outcomes

### Victory Scenarios
- **Complete Vindication**: Reed is cleared, conspiracy exposed, crew becomes heroes (but makes powerful enemies)
- **Pyrrhic Victory**: Reed is cleared but dies/disappears, truth partially revealed, crew must go into hiding
- **Compromise Victory**: Reed convicted of lesser charges, conspiracy contained, crew paid to stay quiet

### Defeat Scenarios
- **Noble Defeat**: Reed is convicted but the crew's efforts expose enough truth to prevent war
- **Sacrificial Defeat**: Reed takes full blame to protect others, crew learns valuable information for future use
- **Total Defeat**: Reed is executed, crew is discredited, conspiracy continues

### Twist Endings
- **Reed Was Right But Also Wrong**: The bioweapon was real, but Reed also committed actual crimes
- **The Greater Good**: The conspiracy is revealed to be preventing an even worse catastrophe
- **Everyone Is Lying**: Multiple conspiracies are using the trial for their own purposes
- **The Long Game**: The trial itself is a distraction from the real operation happening elsewhere

- Technical testimony

### Legal Precedents
- The Kinunir Incident (military necessity defense)
- Regina vs. Blackstone (civilian collateral damage)

## Referee Notes

### Pacing
- Start with straightforward military trial, gradually reveal deeper conspiracy
- Each court session should reveal new information that changes the crew's understanding
- Balance investigation, roleplay, and courtroom drama
- Use time pressure to force difficult decisions

### Themes
- Justice vs. Law
- Individual conscience vs. chain of command
- The price of truth
- Loyalty and betrayal
- The complexity of moral choices in war

### Adaptability
- Can be scaled from simple military trial to sector-spanning conspiracy
- NPCs can be adjusted based on crew's existing relationships
- Evidence can be tailored to crew's skills and interests
- Outcome should reflect crew's choices and priorities
