# Savage World Research Facility
*Horror Survival Adventure Concept*

## Core Concept

The Aros Cognitive Research Station sits isolated on Vendetta Prime, a savage world whose aggressive megafauna provides the perfect cover for consciousness transfer experiments that would be illegal anywhere in Imperial space. This adventure draws heavy inspiration from Jurassic Park, but the horror runs deeper: the facility isn't studying the creatures themselves, but using their neural complexity for forbidden research into consciousness mapping and transfer protocols.

The facility represents humanity's darkest ambitions disguised as xenobiological research. Officially, they're studying predator behavior patterns. Unofficially, they're attempting to map and transfer human consciousness into alien neural architectures, believing the savage creatures' highly developed hunting instincts and neural plasticity make ideal vessels. When containment fails, the escaped specimens aren't just dangerous predators—some carry fragmented human consciousness, creating horrifying hybrids of human intelligence and alien instinct.

## Setting and Atmosphere

Vendetta Prime is a garden world gone wrong. While technically habitable with a breathable atmosphere and comfortable temperature ranges, the planet's ecosystem developed along extraordinarily aggressive lines. The local fauna includes pack-hunting raptors the size of ground cars, ambush predators that can camouflage perfectly with their surroundings, and massive apex predators that can tear through reinforced walls. But Aros Corporation didn't choose this world for the creatures—they chose it for isolation and the creatures' unusually developed neural structures that make them compatible with human synaptic patterns.

The facility itself is a sprawling complex of interconnected domes and corridors, built to TL-12 standards with multiple redundant containment systems. The upper levels maintain the facade of xenobiological research with observation labs and specimen habitats. But the real work happens in the subterranean neural mapping laboratories, where rows of neural interface pods connect to holding pens via miles of fiber-optic neural conduits. The administrative wing houses luxurious quarters for senior staff who are unaware of the true research, while the secured underground sections require special clearance. Most critically, the shuttle pad sits exposed on the facility's edge, connected by a single reinforced causeway that becomes a killing field when specimens with human tactical knowledge escape.

## The Cast of Characters

**Dr. Elias Harrington**, the Facility Director, knows about the xenobiology research but not the consciousness experiments happening in the restricted levels. He embodies well-meaning ignorance, consistently downplaying dangers because he doesn't understand the true nature of what his facility contains. His growing paranoia about restricted areas he can't access will either make him an ally or a liability when the truth emerges.

**Dr. Sarah Chen**, the Lead Xenobiologist, serves as the facility's public face, genuinely believing she's studying predator behavior. Unknown to her, the neural mapping data she collects feeds directly into the consciousness transfer project below. Her expertise with the creatures becomes invaluable during the crisis, but learning the truth about her research's application may break her.

**Dr. Viktor Aros**, secretly on-site as "Dr. Smith" from the neurology department, is the architect of the consciousness transfer project. He's already successfully transferred his dying wife's consciousness into a specimen, though the result is a horrifying failure he keeps in a special containment cell. He'll do anything to protect his research, viewing the breach as an opportunity to conduct field tests.

**Marcus Thompson**, the Security Chief, is a competent ex-marine who's noticed troubling patterns: personnel who enter restricted areas returning with subtle personality changes, specimens exhibiting unusually coordinated behavior, and staff members who've simply vanished. His investigation threatens to expose everything, making him a target even before the breach.

**Janet "Jax" Rodriguez**, a maintenance technician, has accidentally discovered the neural conduit network while conducting repairs. She knows something unnatural connects the specimens to the lower levels but hasn't pieced together the full horror. Her knowledge of these hidden connections becomes crucial when survivors need alternate routes through the facility.

**Test Subject 23**, formerly Lieutenant Commander Rebecca Hayes, volunteered for consciousness transfer to escape court martial. Her mind now inhabits a pack leader specimen, retaining enough human intelligence to be terrifyingly strategic but driven by alien instincts. She remembers faces, understands human tactics, and harbors a very human desire for revenge against her captors.

## The Cascading Disaster

The breach begins subtly during what seems like a routine day. The player characters have arrived for whatever mission brought them here: delivering specialized equipment, transporting VIP personnel, or investigating missed check-ins. Initial signs include flickering lights dismissed as generator maintenance, unusual agitation among smaller specimens, and security sensors showing false positives.

The first real incident involves the escape of smaller pack hunters from Lab 3. Security manages to contain them, but the gunfire and chaos masks the real problem: the primary containment system is failing systematically. Someone or something has introduced a virus into the facility's computer systems, causing containment fields to fail in sequence from least dangerous to most dangerous specimens, suggesting intelligent design rather than random failure.

When the apex predators escape, they immediately destroy the primary shuttle in a display of terrifying intelligence, cutting off the most obvious escape route. The main communications array falls next, whether from creature damage or sabotage. Emergency protocols activate, sealing sections of the facility, but this traps survivors with predators as often as it provides safety.

## Survival Horror Elements

The adventure emphasizes resource management and escalating tension. Ammunition is limited to what security had on-hand for "routine wildlife encounters." The medical bay is on the wrong side of a breached section. The armory requires authorization codes from someone who's already been killed. Each bullet fired, stimpack used, or battery depleted can't be easily replaced.

Environmental hazards compound the creature threat. The facility's automated systems work against survivors as much as for them: doors seal at inopportune moments, elevators stop between floors, and life support begins failing in sealed sections. Emergency lighting casts everything in hellish red glows that make it impossible to distinguish blood from shadows.

## The Escape Plan

The backup shuttle in Maintenance Bay 7 hasn't flown in two years. Its drives need recalibration, the fuel cells are depleted, and critical components were cannibalized for the primary shuttle's last overhaul. Survivors must venture into creature-infested areas to retrieve parts: navigation computers from the destroyed primary shuttle, fuel cells from the generator station, and a intact flight recorder from the administrative wing's emergency pod.

Each retrieval mission becomes a tense expedition where stealth might serve better than firepower. The creatures learn from each encounter, adapting their hunting strategies. What worked once won't work twice. Time pressure mounts as the facility's fusion reactor, damaged in the initial chaos, counts down to either shutdown (leaving everyone in darkness) or overload (destroying everything in a kilometer radius).

## Climax and Resolution

The final escape requires holding the maintenance bay while final repairs are completed. Waves of creatures assault the position while someone must manually override the bay doors from an exposed control station. Not everyone will make it. The shuttle, even repaired, can't carry everyone. Moral choices about who boards first become life-and-death decisions made in seconds.

The launch itself provides no guarantee of safety. Larger flying predators pursue the shuttle into the atmosphere. The damaged vehicle might need creative piloting to reach orbit. Even in space, the adventure's consequences follow the survivors: corporate assassination teams, xenomorphic contamination, or the horrible realization that someone aboard caused the breach and has their own agenda for the rescued research data.
