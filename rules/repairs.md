# Starship Repairs Guide

**REPAIRS** = Fixing things that have already broken (costs vary based on damage)

Repairs are needed when systems break from combat, accidents, or failed maintenance. Unlike maintenance, repairs are reactive - they fix problems after they occur.

---

## Types of Damage

### Critical Hits
- **Cause**: Combat, poor maintenance, accidents, system overloads
- **Effect**: Specific system malfunction or failure
- **Repair**: Requires Engineer/Mechanic skill + spare parts
- **Examples**: Weapon jammed, drive offline, life support failing

### Hull Damage  
- **Cause**: Combat, collisions, explosive decompression
- **Effect**: Loss of Hull points, potential atmosphere loss
- **Repair**: Requires Mechanic skill + spare parts
- **Examples**: Hull breaches, structural damage, armor loss

---

## Critical Hit Repairs

### Temporary Repairs (Jury-Rigging)
**Purpose**: Get broken system working temporarily

**Requirements:**
- **No parts required** - uses improvised materials
- **Time**: As per system (usually 1 combat round to several minutes)
- **Duration**: System works for 1D hours before failing again
- **Skill**: Engineer or appropriate specialty (e.g., Engineer (j-drive) for jump drive)

**Typical Jury-Rigging Actions:**
- Bypass damaged circuits
- Reroute power/fuel lines  
- Patch holes with improvised materials
- Override safety systems temporarily

### Permanent Repairs
**Purpose**: Properly fix the broken system

**Requirements:**
- **Time**: 1D hours of work
- **Skill**: Engineer or Mechanic check
- **Parts**: Spare parts based on skill check success

**Spare Parts Required:**

| Engineer/Mechanic Effect | Spare Parts Required |
|---------------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | 1.0 tons |
| 2 | 0.8 tons |
| 3 | 0.6 tons |
| 4 | 0.4 tons |
| 5 | 0.2 tons |
| 6+ | None (expert repair) |

**Note**: Critical Hit Severity can modify spare parts needed (subtract Severity from Effect)

### Where Critical Hits Can Be Repaired
- **Anywhere**: If you have skilled crew, tools, and parts
- **Starports**: Professional repair services available (costs vary by class)
- **Emergency**: Field repairs with improvised materials (jury-rigging only)

---

## Hull Damage Repairs

### Requirements
- **Time**: 1 hour per Hull point restored
- **Skill**: Routine (6+) Mechanic check (INT or EDU)  
- **Parts**: 1 ton spare parts per Hull point
- **Tools**: Hull patches, welding equipment, structural materials

### Procedure
1. **Assess damage**: Determine extent of structural damage
2. **Prepare area**: Clear debris, ensure safe working conditions
3. **Make repairs**: Cut, weld, patch as needed
4. **Test integrity**: Pressure test, structural stress test

### Where Hull Repairs Can Be Done
- **Class A/B Starports**: Full structural repair capabilities
- **Class C Starports**: Basic hull repair possible  
- **Class D Starports**: Emergency patches only
- **Ship/Field**: If crew has proper tools and parts

---

## Repair Facilities by Starport Class

### Class A Starport (Excellent)
- **Critical Hits**: Any severity, any system, any TL
- **Hull Damage**: Complete structural reconstruction possible
- **Parts Available**: Any TL components, custom orders
- **Time**: Professional speed with full crews
- **Cost**: Standard Imperial rates

### Class B Starport (Good)  
- **Critical Hits**: Most systems repairable up to high TL
- **Hull Damage**: Full hull reconstruction capabilities
- **Parts Available**: Most TL available (may import high-TL)
- **Time**: Good professional service
- **Cost**: Standard rates, slight premium for imports

### Class C Starport (Routine)
- **Critical Hits**: Basic systems, standard TL components
- **Hull Damage**: Minor hull repairs, patches
- **Parts Available**: Standard components only (TL 9-11 typically)
- **Time**: Slower service, limited crews
- **Cost**: Variable, often higher than standard

### Class D Starport (Poor)
- **Critical Hits**: Emergency fixes only, basic systems
- **Hull Damage**: Temporary patches, basic welding
- **Parts Available**: Very limited, mostly salvaged
- **Time**: Slow if available at all
- **Cost**: High for minimal service

### Class E/X Starport (Frontier/None)
- **No professional repair facilities**
- **Crew must perform all repairs**
- **No spare parts available for purchase**  
- **Field/emergency repairs only**

## Tools and Equipment for Repairs

### Basic Repair Tools (minimum for any repair work)
**Standard Engineer Tool Set:**
- Multispectral vision goggles
- Iris valve opener
- Electronic and gravitic testing equipment  
- Soldering gear and micromanipulators
- Pliers, wrenches, sockets, mallet/gravity hammer
- Hull patches and portable airlock

**Additional Basic Equipment:**
- Spare parts inventory
- Portable welding/cutting equipment
- Emergency hull patches
- Portable power supply

### Advanced Repair Equipment

**Repair Drones:**
- **Function**: Allow exterior repairs during combat/dangerous conditions  
- **Skill Required**: Electronics (remote ops)
- **Effective Skill**: Level 1 or operator skill (whichever lower)  
- **Limitations**: Cannot replace destroyed equipment, only repair

**Workshop:**
- **Function**: Manufacture parts from raw materials
- **Benefit**: Reduces spare parts inventory needs
- **Requirements**: Raw materials, power, skilled technician

**Machine Shop:**
- **Function**: Complex component fabrication and precision work
- **Benefit**: Can create custom parts, modify existing components
- **Requirements**: High-TL manufacturing equipment

## Spare Parts

### Cost and Availability
- **Standard Cost**: Cr100,000 per ton
- **Class A/B Starports**: Always available, any TL
- **Class C Starports**: Usually available, may need to wait for delivery
- **Class D Starports**: Limited availability, expensive
- **Class E/X Starports**: Not available

### Types of Spare Parts
- **General Components**: Universal fittings, electronics, mechanical parts
- **System-Specific**: Drive components, weapon parts, life support elements  
- **High-TL Components**: Advanced materials, gravitic elements, computer cores
- **Emergency Supplies**: Hull patches, emergency power cells, atmosphere canisters

### Inventory Management
- **Minimum for Independence**: 1-2 tons for basic operations
- **Recommended for Extended Missions**: 3-4 tons  
- **With Workshop**: Can reduce inventory (manufacture from raw materials)
- **Specialized Ships**: May need specific spare parts for unique systems

## Emergency Repair Procedures

### Field Repairs
**When proper facilities are unavailable:**

1. **Damage Assessment**
   - Use ship's sensors for internal damage
   - Visual inspection for hull breaches
   - System diagnostics for critical hits

2. **Resource Inventory**
   - Available spare parts
   - Improvised materials from cargo/ship systems
   - Tool availability and crew skills

3. **Priority Triage**
   - Focus on life-threatening issues first
   - Essential systems over convenience
   - Temporary fixes to reach proper facilities

4. **Execution**
   - Implement safest repairs first
   - Document all emergency repairs
   - Plan for proper repairs at next opportunity

### Repair Priority System
**When resources are limited:**

1. **Life Support**: Atmosphere, pressure, temperature control
2. **Jump Drive**: Ability to reach help/escape danger  
3. **Manoeuvre Drive**: Basic ship mobility and docking
4. **Power Plant**: Powers all other systems
5. **Navigation/Communications**: Finding help, calling for assistance
6. **Weapons/Defensive Systems**: Protection during repairs
7. **Cargo/Comfort Systems**: Lowest priority

### Improvised Materials
- **Hull Patches**: Cargo containers, interior panels, deck plating
- **Seals**: Rubber cargo strapping, emergency sealant
- **Wiring**: Lighting circuits, non-essential system cables
- **Structural Support**: Cargo handling equipment, furniture
- **Pressure Barriers**: Airlocks, blast doors, emergency bulkheads

## Repair Costs vs Maintenance Costs

### Maintenance (Prevention)
- **Free Trader (MCr37)**: Cr37,000 annual OR Cr3,083 monthly
- **Predictable**: Same cost every period
- **Prevents problems**: Avoids repair situations

### Repairs (After Failure)  
- **Minor Critical Hit**: 0.2-1.0 tons parts (Cr20,000-100,000)
- **Major Critical Hit**: 0.8-1.0 tons parts (Cr80,000-100,000)  
- **Hull Point Repair**: 1 ton parts (Cr100,000) + labor costs
- **Unpredictable**: Costs vary based on damage severity
- **Plus Labor**: Starport repair services charge additional fees

**Key Point**: Regular maintenance is cheaper than emergency repairs.

---

## Combat Repairs

### During Combat
- **Jury-rigging only**: No time for permanent repairs
- **Damage Control**: Focus on keeping ship functional
- **Priority Systems**: Life support, propulsion, weapons
- **Repair Drones**: Allow exterior repairs under fire

### Post-Combat
- **Damage Assessment**: Complete system check
- **Immediate Needs**: Life support, hull breaches
- **Temporary Repairs**: Get to safe harbor
- **Permanent Repairs**: At appropriate facility

---

## Insurance and Legal Considerations

### Insurance Coverage
- **Combat Damage**: May be excluded from standard policies
- **Negligent Maintenance**: Can void repair coverage
- **Proper Documentation**: Required for claims
- **Approved Facilities**: Some insurers require certified repair shops

### Imperial Regulations  
- **Safety Standards**: Repairs must meet Imperial codes
- **Inspection Requirements**: Major repairs may require certification
- **Record Keeping**: Maintenance and repair logs mandatory
- **Hazardous Materials**: Special procedures for drive components
