# Session 3: The Scrap Vultures - Rech, 1105

The old spacer had made his way to the Broken Hull, a tavern carved into the guts of a decommissioned ore hauler on Wypoc Station. The place still reeked of processed metals and desperation, fitting for a story about Rech. He'd commandeered a booth near the emergency airlock—always good to have options—and was nursing something the bartender claimed was whiskey but tasted more like thruster coolant. A group of salvage rats had gathered, their magnetic boots clanking against the deck plates as they leaned in to hear the tale.

"The Stardust Drifter's crew on Rech," he began, swirling the amber poison in his glass. "You want to know about the week that almost ended them before they even reached their prize? This was still before the war, when the Scrap Vultures were known as dangerous salvagers who didn't like competition."

He took a long pull, grimacing at the burn.

"First thing you need to understand about Rech—it wasn't just lawless, it was predatory. Every shopkeeper was a spy, every alley was a trap, and everyone knew everyone else's business before you did. The crew needed vacc suits for the Djinni run, and there was only one place to get them: Vacuum Solutions Ltd. That pristine corporate storefront, all chrome and forced smiles in the middle of hell. Sydney and Kris walked in together, credits burning holes in their pockets.

"They didn't notice the woman with the grease-stained fingers examining emergency seal kits. Zina 'Sparks' Petrova—the Scrap Vultures' tech specialist—cataloging every purchase, every hesitation, every credit transferred. She watched Sydney test the flexibility of a TL10 suit's joints with the kind of attention that only comes from planning to exploit weaknesses later. When they left with two suits, she was already on her comm, reporting to Kara 'Wrench' Riker that the competition was definitely planning EVA work.

"Meanwhile, Aria and Ash were navigating the narrow corridors to Doc Hazel's Medical Exchange. You know the type of place—blood on the floor from the morning's surgery, shelves stocked with everything from field dressings to combat stims that would stop your heart if you looked at them wrong. They stocked up on medkits, anti-rad drugs, the works. But it was Grak they noticed—that massive Vargr enforcer trying to look casual outside, his scarred muzzle twitching every time they moved. The vulture logo on his combat knife sheath? Clear as a targeting laser. Aria memorized his stance, the way he favored his left leg, filed it away for later.

"The crew mentioned their shadows to Kaelen that night. Smart woman, that captain—she immediately hired dock guards to watch the *Void Gambit*. Cost her a fortune, but she knew the game. 'If someone's watching you shop,' she said, 'they're planning to take what you bought.'

"Kris needed an edge for what was coming, so he went under the knife—neural augmentation at a black clinic, the kind that burns combat reflexes straight into your synapses. Took three days to recover, his hands shaking from the new pathways being carved through his brain. When he could finally hold a gun steady, he and Sydney hit Korvac's Military Surplus.

"That's where they spotted Joric 'Slick' Fen making a scene, arguing about armor prices loud enough to wake the dead on Regina. But Sydney caught him—those quick glances, the way he shifted to keep them in peripheral vision. Classic surveillance, amateur hour. Sydney picked up a gauss rifle that could punch through a hull, Kris grabbed beam weapons and a stunner. They played it cool, but now they knew: the Scrap Vultures weren't just watching, they were tracking every purchase, building a profile.

"Aria found what she needed at the Circuit Breaker—computer intrusion gear, engineering toolkits, the kind of equipment you need when you're breaking into a dead ship's systems. Sydney grabbed mechanics tools there too. Every shop, every purchase, the Vultures had eyes on them. By the time Aria picked up a laser pistol at Korvac's second location, the crew had mapped out their surveillance network. Four Vultures, all sporting that same logo—a carrion bird clutching salvage tools.

"They tried to wait it out, hoping Kaelen's Navy contacts would give them an edge on patrol schedules. Three days they sat in that pressure cooker of a port, feeling the walls close in. When the intel finally came through—patrol routes, timing windows, the works—they knew they couldn't wait any longer.

"It happened on the walk back to their hotel from the Alibi. A nothing street, the kind you'd pass through a thousand times without thinking. But Kris knew—something about the way the shadows fell wasn't right. He spotted the Vargr first, Grak's bulk poorly concealed behind a dumpster like a cruiser trying to hide behind an asteroid. Then Sydney caught the glint—sniper on the fire escape, rifle already shouldered.

"They dove into a warehouse, Aria's fingers flying over the electronic lock like she was playing a symphony. The mechanism yielded to her enhanced warfare protocols in seconds. Sydney checked their six—two more Vultures converging, moving with the kind of coordination that meant radio contact and practiced tactics.

"They barricaded the door just as something heavy slammed into it from outside. Then came the grenade—smoke or gas, they never found out which—hissing through the front windows like a viper's promise. They found the service ladder to the mezzanine, discovered a window that opened onto a parallel alley. Classic escape route, the kind every building on Rech had because everyone needed one eventually.

"The crew went full evasion protocol after that—doubling back, false trails, entering buildings through one door and leaving through three others. By the time they reached the hotel, they were certain they'd lost their shadows. They packed everything in under ten minutes, made the call to Kaelen: 'We're burned. Launch now or never.'

"The guards Kaelen hired earned their credits that night. The Scrap Vultures showed up at the docking bay, found four barrels pointed at them, and suddenly remembered urgent business elsewhere. The *Void Gambit* was already cycling her engines when the crew arrived, their purchases hastily stowed, that fresh intel from Kaelen's contacts loaded into the nav computer.

"But as they burned for the jump point, sensors picked up another ship lighting drives behind them. The *Scrap Vulture* herself—ugly as her crew, all welded armor plates and oversized engines. She was hammering those drives past redline, the thermal bloom visible to half the system. They were trying for an intercept, but the *Void Gambit* had the angle, the head start, and something else—Kaelen's rarely-shown modifications. The ship moved like liquid mercury, her true speed finally unleashed, leaving the Vultures eating exotic particles.

"Then the *Scrap Vulture* did something insane, even for pirates—jumped at 85 diameters instead of the safe 100. You know what that means? Fifty-fifty chance of coming out the other side as atoms scattered across jump space. But they made it, the lucky bastards, already heading for Djinni while the *Void Gambit* played it safe, took the full run to 100 diameters.

"The Imperial Navy hailed them as soon as they entered Djinni space—standard interdiction warning, turn back or be fired upon. Kaelen just smiled, triggered the false transponder they'd prepared. Made them look like a scheduled maintenance drone, invisible to Navy sensors for just long enough. They found the derelict right where it should be, the *Stardust Drifter* floating dark and silent among the rocks.

"But the docking rings were slag—twisted metal and melted composites. Sydney went EVA to fix it, nothing but vacuum suit and determination between him and the infinite dark. That's when the *Scrap Vulture* announced herself, drives flaring as she closed the distance. She'd been running dark, waiting for them to commit to the salvage.

"Sydney worked those repairs like a man possessed while Kris floated supplies across the gap. They got minimal power online, just enough for atmosphere in one compartment. Aria and Ash made the crossing while Kaelen positioned the *Void Gambit* at maximum weapons range—close enough to support, far enough to make the Vultures think twice about boarding.

"Mexican standoff at the edge of nowhere. The Scrap Vultures couldn't board without eating fire from the *Void Gambit*, and Kaelen wasn't about to abandon the Stardust Drifter's crew she'd been hired to protect. Everything hanging on who would blink first, while the Imperial Navy patrol counted down the hours until they arrived."

The old spacer stood, dropping credits on the scarred table.

"That's where it stood when I last heard this part of the tale—the Stardust Drifter's crew split between two ships, the Vultures circling like their namesake, and the Navy bearing down on all of them. The kind of moment that separates the legends from the debris fields."

He headed for the airlock, pausing at the threshold.

"Sometimes I wonder if that augmentation Kris got was what saved them, or if it was just pure stubborn refusal to let the vultures win. Either way, they were about to learn what was hidden in that ghost ship—and why someone was willing to hire pirates to get it back."

The airlock cycled closed, leaving the salvage rats to their drinks and dreams of treasures that might be worth dying for, back before the war taught everyone that some things were worth even more than that.
