# Session 1: The Inheritance - Regina, 1105

The grizzled spacer settled into the cracked booth at the back of Murphy's Landing, a dive bar three levels down from the main concourse of Efate Station. The amber lighting hadn't been upgraded since the Third Frontier War, and the ancient air recyclers wheezed like dying men. He signaled the bartender for another Solomani whiskey—the real stuff, imported before the trade sanctions—and fixed his gaze on the young free traders nursing their synthetic ales.

"You want to hear about the Star Duster's crew?" His voice carried that distinctive rasp of too many decompression incidents. "This was back in ought-five, before the Fifth Frontier War turned the Marches into a graveyard. Back when we thought Makhidkarun was just another megacorp playing market games, not the tentacled monster it turned out to be."

He took a long pull from his glass.

"It started with six letters, delivered simultaneously across three systems. That's not coincidence—that's orchestrated. Some xenoarchaeologist named Aris had died, left them a ship called the *Stardust Drifter*. Problem was, the ship was sitting dead in the Djinni system, which had just been slapped with a Red Zone designation by the Navy. Quarantined. Shoot-on-sight orders for unauthorized vessels. But the Star Duster's crew? They saw opportunity where others saw suicide.

"First to hit Regina dirtside was the quiet one—moved through customs like smoke through fingers. Never saw a ghost walk so casual past security scanners that could read your DNA from your breath. Then came the soldier, marching in like he still had a platoon at his back. The others trickled in over three days: the smooth-talker who could sell jump coordinates to a Vilani, the tech with more chrome in his skull than regulations allowed, the pilot who flew like physics was just a suggestion, and the medic who'd seen too much to be surprised by anything.

"They bunked at the Celestial Plaza—you know, that chrome monstrosity with the holographic waterfalls? Cost more per night than a deckhand makes in a month. Security pulled the smooth-talker aside for a 'random' sweep. Random my ass—someone had flagged them already. But while security was playing twenty questions with him, the medic was working her network. Old connections from her Navy days, before that incident at Jewell that got classified so hard they invented new classifications for it. What she heard made their blood run cold: every megacorp from Regina to Core had blacklisted Dr. Aris in his final months. Cut him off like a gangrenous limb. That kind of coordination? That's fear. Pure, crystallized corporate fear.

"The meet was on the 87th floor of Commerce Tower. Haskins & Associates—all real wood paneling and leather that probably cost more than the *Stardust Drifter* herself. The soldier caught it first, little tells that the lawyer was sweating through his thousand-credit suit. Documents sliding across the table too fast, eyes that wouldn't meet theirs. The quiet one, though—he'd studied Imperial salvage law during the jump to Regina. Started quoting precedents from the Second Survey, subsection this, paragraph that. The lawyer cracked like cheap ceramic under pressure.

"Turns out the law firm was washing their hands of Aris entirely. The ship's title? They practically threw it at them. 'Just take it,' the lawyer said, 'and never mention our firm's involvement.' The transponder codes, the berth records, all of it. Ship was in Djinni all right, somewhere in that asteroid soup they call a belt. But here's the kicker—they'd have to file salvage claims in K'Kirka before anyone would recognize their ownership. Imperial bureaucracy at its finest.

"But the real prize was the data wafer. The soldier cracked it open right there—no sense giving their enemies time to prepare countermeasures. Message was short: 'Find Roric Vance. Tell him the Whiskey Jack needs a fast ship.' The tech tried to slice deeper into the wafer's architecture, looking for hidden partitions, but whoever encrypted it was paranoid even by intelligence standards. Dead-man switches nested inside kill-codes wrapped in polymorphic ice. One wrong probe and the whole thing would've melted into quantum noise.

"Finding Roric Vance in Regina Downport? That's like finding a specific hydrogen atom in a gas giant. The pilot, though—he knew how to navigate the gray zones. Every port has them, those liminal spaces where legal meets illegal and shakes hands. Led them straight to The Gravity Well, a dive so far down in the port structure that gravity actually felt heavier. Old TL-11 aesthetic—exposed conduits dripping condensation, CRT displays showing departure schedules in that green phosphor glow, mechanical keyboards that clacked like typewriters. The kind of place where the beer tastes like recycled coolant and nobody uses their real name.

"Vance was there all right, tucked into the darkest corner booth like a man trying to disappear into the shadows. Shaking hands, bloodshot eyes, the sweet reek of metabolized alcohol seeping from his pores. The medic dropped the code phrase casual as ordering another round: 'The Whiskey Jack needs a fast ship.'

"You should've seen Vance's face—like watching a man remember he used to be somebody. The smooth-talker worked him gentle but firm, and Vance spilled it all. Two options for getting to Djinni: Captain Kaelen of *The Void Gambit* for two million credits—professional, reliable, expensive. Or Rico 'Hotshot' Vega and his *Stray Comet*—cheaper, faster, and a fifty-fifty chance of dying in a fireball. But then Vance dropped the real payload: Silas Kane had his hooks in this. The Gilded Kane himself, with his Makhidkarun backing. And there was something on the *Stardust Drifter's* data core, something called the Kane Contingency that had half the subsector's shadow players breaking out in cold sweats.

"Now, legitimate banks don't lend three million credits on salvage claims in Red Zones. That's where Kane came in. The Gilded Cage—his 'pawn shop'—sat in mid-port like a spider in a web of credit and debt. The quiet one tried to dig up leverage before the meet, but Kane's records were cleaner than a Navy dress uniform. When they sat across from him—this mountain of a man in a suit that cost more than most ships' annual maintenance—the tech tried to negotiate. Might as well try to negotiate with a black hole.

"The terms Kane offered weren't just criminal—they were designed to fail. Forty percent monthly interest. Sixty days to pay. Every asset they'd ever own as collateral. The crew knew they were being played, but what choice did they have? The pilot, sharp-eyed bastard, caught something in subsection twelve of the third addendum—a renegotiation clause triggered by 'extraordinary circumstances.' Kane had left himself an out, which meant he expected things to go sideways.

"As they were leaving, three million in untraceable credit chips weighing down their pockets, Kane mentioned—casual as commenting on the weather—that the Rust Dogs might've heard about their little venture. Kara 'Wrench' Riker's crew, the nastiest salvage pirates in three subsectors. Kane was already hedging his bets, playing every angle like a three-dimensional chess master.

"But here's what Kane didn't know—what none of them knew yet. The *Stardust Drifter* wasn't just some lab ship. It was Aris's magnum opus, and buried in its data core was enough dirt to bury Makhidkarun's operations in the entire subsector. The Kane Contingency wasn't just leverage—it was a nuclear option. And the Star Duster's crew had just signed up to retrieve it from a quarantined system patrolled by the Imperial Navy, with corporate assassins and salvage pirates racing them to the prize."

The old spacer drained his whiskey and stood, steadying himself against the table.

"That was day one on Regina. Six strangers became a crew, accepted a debt that could crush a small corporation, and set course for a Red Zone with nothing but a ship's title and a prayer. They didn't know they were about to become legends. They just knew they couldn't afford to fail."

He flipped a credit chip to the bartender and headed for the door, his footsteps echoing in the bar's recycled air.

"Next time you see me, buy me a drink and I'll tell you about the run to Djinni. About how they turned a corvette captain's strict adherence to regulations into an escape route. About the firefight in the asteroid belt when the Rust Dogs came calling. About how they used the Kane Contingency to flip the entire game board and walk away free and clear."

The door sealed behind him with a pneumatic hiss, leaving the young traders to contemplate their synthetic ales and wonder if they'd ever have a story worth telling in a bar like this.
