# Session 1: The Inheritance - Regina, 1105

Fifty years. That's how long it had been since the events the grizzled spacer was about to relate, as he settled into the cracked booth at the back of Murphy's Landing, a dive bar three levels down from the main concourse of Efate Station. Half a century since the Stardust Drifter's crew walked the decks of Regina Station, back when the Fifth Frontier War was just a storm gathering on the horizon that nobody saw coming. The amber lighting hadn't been upgraded since the Third Frontier War, and the ancient air recyclers wheezed like dying men. He signaled the bartender for another Solomani whiskey—the real stuff, imported before the trade sanctions—and fixed his gaze on the young free traders nursing their synthetic ales.

"You want to hear about the Stardust Drifter's crew?" His voice carried that distinctive rasp of too many decompression incidents. "This was back before the Fifth Frontier War turned the Marches into a graveyard. Back when we thought certain megacorps were just playing market games, not the tentacled monsters they turned out to be."

He took a long pull from his glass.

"It started with five letters, delivered simultaneously across three systems. That's not coincidence—that's orchestrated. Some xenoarchaeologist named Aros had left them a ship called the *Stardust Drifter*. Problem was, the ship was sitting dead in the Djinni system, which had been under Red Zone designation for years now. Quarantined. Shoot-on-sight orders for unauthorized vessels. But the Stardust Drifter's crew? They saw opportunity where others saw suicide.

"They arrived on Regina over the course of three days. Five strangers converging on the highport, each carrying the same encrypted summons. Different gates, different times, but all heading for the same destination.

"They bunked at the Celestial Plaza—you know, that chrome monstrosity with the holographic waterfalls? Cost more per night than a deckhand makes in a month. Security pulled one of them aside for a 'random' sweep. Random my ass—someone had flagged them already. But while security was playing twenty questions, the others were working their networks. Old connections, favors called in. What they heard made their blood run cold: every megacorp from Regina to Core had blacklisted Dr. Aros before he disappeared. Cut him off like a gangrenous limb. That kind of coordination? That's fear. Pure, crystallized corporate fear.

"The meet was on the 87th floor of Commerce Tower. Haskins & Associates—all real wood paneling and leather that probably cost more than the *Stardust Drifter* herself. Mike caught it first, little tells that the lawyer was sweating through his thousand-credit suit. Documents sliding across the table too fast, eyes that wouldn't meet theirs. Ash, though—with his scholar's education, he'd studied Imperial salvage law during the jump to Regina. Started quoting precedents from the Second Survey, subsection this, paragraph that. The lawyer cracked like cheap ceramic under pressure.

"Turns out the law firm was washing their hands of Aros entirely. The ship's title? They practically threw it at them. 'Just take it,' the lawyer said, 'and never mention our firm's involvement.' The transponder codes, the berth records, all of it. Ship was in Djinni all right, somewhere in that asteroid soup they call a belt. But here's the kicker—they'd have to file salvage claims in K'Kirka before anyone would recognize their ownership. Imperial bureaucracy at its finest.

"But the real prize was the data wafer. Ash cracked it open right there—no sense giving their enemies time to prepare countermeasures. Message was short: 'Find Roric Vance. Tell him the Whiskey Jack needs a fast ship.' Sydney tried to slice deeper into the wafer's architecture, looking for hidden partitions, but whoever encrypted it was paranoid even by intelligence standards. Dead-man switches nested inside kill-codes wrapped in polymorphic ice. One wrong probe and the whole thing would've melted into quantum noise.

"Finding Roric Vance in Regina Downport? That's like finding a specific hydrogen atom in a gas giant. Sydney, though—with his years in the shadier side of space, he knew how to navigate the gray zones. Every port has them, those liminal spaces where legal meets illegal and shakes hands. Led them straight to The Gravity Well, a dive so far down in the port structure that gravity actually felt heavier. Old TL-11 aesthetic—exposed conduits dripping condensation, CRT displays showing departure schedules in that green phosphor glow, mechanical keyboards that clacked like typewriters. The kind of place where the beer tastes like recycled coolant and nobody uses their real name.

"Vance was there all right, tucked into the darkest corner booth like a man trying to disappear into the shadows. Shaking hands, bloodshot eyes, the sweet reek of metabolized alcohol seeping from his pores. Someone dropped the code phrase casual as ordering another round: 'Whiskey Jack needs a fast ship.'

"You should've seen Vance's face—like watching a man remember he used to be somebody. Aria worked him gentle but firm, and Vance started to spill. Two options for getting to Djinni, he said: Captain Kaelen of *The Void Gambit* for two million credits—professional, reliable, expensive. Or Rico 'Hotshot' Vega and his *Stray Comet*—cheaper, faster, and a fifty-fifty chance of dying in a fireball. 

"The conversation was still unfolding when I left that night. The crew huddled around Vance's booth, weighing their options, calculating risks against their dwindling credits. They'd need financing, that much was clear. Three million credits don't just materialize from thin air, not for a crew of strangers who'd just inherited a ship trapped in a Red Zone.

"But even then, you could see it in their eyes—that spark that separates the legends from the corpses. They weren't going to walk away from this. Not Ash with his scholarly determination, not Sydney with his pilot's hunger for one more score, not Aria with her military precision, not Mina with her noble's pride, not Mike with his unshakeable resolve. They were going to find a way to that ship, or die trying."

The old spacer drained his whiskey and stood, steadying himself against the table.

"That was the beginning on Regina. Five strangers starting to become a crew, weighing impossible choices in a dive bar at the bottom of the port. They didn't know they were about to become legends. Hell, they didn't even know how they'd scrape together the credits yet. But you could already tell—these weren't the kind to walk away from a challenge."

He flipped a credit chip to the bartender and headed for the door, his footsteps echoing in the bar's recycled air.

"Next time you see me, buy me a drink and I'll tell you what happened next. How they found the credits, who they had to deal with to get them, and how they managed to hire a pilot crazy enough to run a Red Zone blockade."

The door sealed behind him with a pneumatic hiss, leaving the young traders to contemplate their synthetic ales and wonder if they'd ever have a story worth telling in a bar like this.
