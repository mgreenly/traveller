# Aesthetic Characteristics

This document describes the aesthetic characteristics of various elements in our game universe. These descriptions serve as a consistent reference when writing fiction, creating descriptions, or establishing the visual and sensory qualities of the setting. Maintaining consistent aesthetics helps immerse players in the universe and reinforces the technological and cultural distinctions between different tech levels and civilizations.

## Starships

### TL-11 Starships

TL-11 starships embody the "used universe" aesthetic pioneered by industrial science fiction of the late 20th century. These vessels feature a distinctly functional, blue-collar design philosophy where form follows function with brutal efficiency. The interiors are characterized by exposed conduits, heavy-duty manual switches, and tactile control interfaces with chunky, mechanical keyboards and analog gauges. Steel mesh grating forms walkways over maintenance corridors, while steam vents and coolant pipes run along bulkheads painted in utilitarian grays and industrial yellows. Computer displays are low-resolution CRTs with monochrome or limited color palettes, displaying information in blocky text and simple vector graphics. The atmosphere is one of retrofitted technology - layers of modifications and repairs visible in mismatched panels and jury-rigged systems. Emergency lighting consists of rotating beacons and harsh industrial strobes, while main lighting comes from caged fluorescent fixtures that cast hard shadows in the perpetual twilight of the engineering spaces. These ships feel lived-in and worn, with scuff marks on handrails, oil stains on deck plating, and the constant background hum of life support systems and hydraulic actuators. The bridge might feature rows of physical toggle switches, analog meters, and banks of blinking status lights, while warning klaxons are mechanical bells and buzzers rather than synthesized tones.

### TL-15 Starships

TL-15 starships represent a quantum leap in both technology and design philosophy, embracing a sleek, minimalist aesthetic that prioritizes elegant efficiency and human-machine integration. These vessels feature smooth, seamless surfaces of advanced composites and smart materials that can adjust their properties on command. Control interfaces are predominantly holographic, with gesture-based commands and neural interface options for enhanced crew. LCARS-style displays provide intuitive, colorful touchscreen interfaces with smooth animations and real-time data visualization. The lighting is ambient and adaptive, using hidden LED strips and photonic panels that adjust color temperature and intensity based on operational status and circadian requirements. Physical controls, where they exist, are flush-mounted capacitive surfaces that illuminate when activated. The bridge resembles a high-tech command center with wraparound holographic displays that can render three-dimensional tactical situations or provide immersive external views. Workstations feature transparent aluminum surfaces with embedded photonic processors, while seating incorporates ergonomic memory materials that adapt to individual crew members. The overall atmosphere is one of controlled precision - climate-controlled environments with advanced air filtration, sound dampening materials that create an almost cathedral-like quiet broken only by subtle harmonic tones for alerts. Corridors feature flowing, organic curves rather than harsh angles, with walls that can become transparent viewports or display surfaces as needed. Even in engineering spaces, the technology is largely solid-state and sealed, with diagnostic holographs replacing the need for physical access panels, and maintenance performed by specialized robots or through molecular-level programmable matter systems.
