# Jump-Trauma: The Psychological Cost of Interstellar Travel

## Overview

Jump-trauma refers to the range of psychological and physiological effects experienced by travelers during and after jump space travel. While jump drives have made interstellar civilization possible, the human mind was never meant to exist outside normal space-time for a week at a time.

## The Jump Experience

According to established jump physics (Core Rules p.157), a ship in jumpspace is:
- **Completely cut off from the universe** - No communication, not even psionic
- **Suspended in a pocket dimension** - A "shimmering bubble of boiling hydrogen"
- **Utterly alone** - Existing in a collapsed parallel universe
- **Time-displaced** - 148+ hours pass regardless of distance traveled

This unnatural state takes a toll on the human psyche that technology cannot fully mitigate.

## Common Symptoms

### During Jump
- **Temporal disorientation** - Loss of circadian rhythms without natural day/night
- **"Jump dreams"** - Vivid hallucinations reported by 23% of conscious travelers
- **Existential anxiety** - Awareness of existing outside normal reality
- **Claustrophobia** - Intensified by knowledge that escape is impossible
- **"The Whispers"** - Auditory hallucinations attributed to hydrogen bubble resonance

### Post-Jump (Jump-Lag)
- **Spatial disorientation** - Difficulty adjusting to new stellar positions
- **Temporal confusion** - Body expects ~7 days to have passed at origin
- **Nausea and vertigo** - Inner ear adjustment to return to normal space
- **"Reality dreams"** - Nightmares about being trapped in jumpspace
- **Hypervigilance** - Obsessive checking that one has returned to normal space

## Risk Factors

### High Risk Groups
- **First-time jumpers** - No developed coping mechanisms
- **Solo travelers** - Lack of social support during jump
- **Frequent jumpers** - Cumulative psychological strain
- **Psionic individuals** - Report sensing the "wrongness" more acutely
- **Combat veterans** - Jump-trauma can trigger existing PTSD

### Protective Factors
- **Experienced crew** - Provide reassurance and distraction
- **Structured routines** - Maintain sense of normalcy
- **Entertainment systems** - Mental engagement reduces anxiety
- **Low berth passage** - Unconsciousness avoids the experience entirely

## Cultural Responses

### Spacer Traditions
- **Jump parties** - Celebrations immediately before jump to boost morale
- **Emergence rituals** - Ceremonies marking return to normal space
- **Jump buddies** - Pairing system for mutual support
- **"Jump juice"** - Various alcohol/stimulant combinations claimed to help
- **Lucky charms** - Talismans believed to ensure safe passage

### Medical Interventions
- **Jump-smooth** - Prescription anxiolytics for the jump-anxious
- **Temporal stabilizers** - Drugs to maintain circadian rhythms
- **VR therapy suites** - Simulated normal environments during jump
- **Psi-shields** - Claimed to block jump-space awareness (unproven)

### Commercial Solutions
- **TerraHome Environments** - Holographic normal-space simulators
- **Stellar Morning™ "JumpLag Special"** - Caffeinated beverage with mild sedatives
- **Zhodani Wellness Centers** - Post-jump psychological adjustment therapy
- **Cosmic Companion AI** - Programmed specifically for jump support

## Long-Term Effects

### Jump Syndrome
Experienced spacers who have made hundreds of jumps sometimes develop:
- **Temporal dissociation** - Feeling "unmoored" from normal time
- **Jump addiction** - Compulsive need to travel through jumpspace
- **Reality questioning** - Persistent doubt about which universe is "real"
- **The Long Dream** - Belief that life is a jump-dream that hasn't ended

### Cultural Impact
Jump-trauma has profoundly influenced Imperial culture:
- Architecture emphasizes windows and open spaces
- Entertainment heavily features "grounding" in reality
- Philosophy grapples with the nature of existence across dimensions
- Religion incorporates jump-space into concepts of afterlife/purgatory

## Treatment Approaches

### Preventive
- **Pre-jump counseling** for first-timers
- **Jump simulation training** in safe environments
- **Crew pairing protocols** on commercial vessels
- **Mandatory recreation schedules** during jump

### Therapeutic
- **Cognitive behavioral therapy** for jump anxiety
- **EMDR** for traumatic jump experiences
- **Group therapy** for frequent travelers
- **Medication** for acute symptoms

### Emergency
- **Jump-abort counseling** for misjump survivors
- **Reality orientation therapy** for severe dissociation
- **Inpatient treatment** for acute jump psychosis

## Game Mechanics: Jump Sickness

### Duration by Passage Type
Upon exiting jump space, all conscious travelers experience jump sickness for:
- **High Passage:** 1d6 hours
- **Middle Passage:** 2d6 hours  
- **Basic Passage:** 3d6 hours
- **Low Passage:** No effect (unconscious during jump)

### Effects
While suffering from jump sickness:
- **DM-1 to all checks using INT, EDU, or SOC**
- **Disorientation:** Difficulty concentrating or performing complex mental tasks
- **Mild nausea:** Character feels unsteady but can still function

### Mitigating Factors
- **Smooth Jump (Engineer):** If the ship's engineer achieves Effect 4+ on their jump check, reduce the number of jump sickness dice by 1:
  - High Passage: 1d6 becomes 0 (no jump sickness)
  - Middle Passage: 2d6 becomes 1d6 hours
  - Basic Passage: 3d6 becomes 2d6 hours
  - Low Passage remains unaffected (already no sickness)
- **Experienced Traveller (10+ jumps):** Reduce duration by 1 hour (minimum 1)
- **Medical treatment:** Medic check (8+) reduces duration by 1d3 hours
- **Jump-smooth drugs:** If taken before jump, halve the duration (round up)
- **First jump ever:** Add 1d6 hours to duration

### Severe Cases
On a natural 12 when rolling duration:
- Character experiences **severe jump sickness**
- **DM-2 to all mental checks** for the duration
- Must make **END 8+** check or be incapacitated for first hour

## Optional Rule: Variable Passage Restocking Costs

While standard rules specify Cr1000 per stateroom for life support and supplies (providing "rather spartan" meals), referees may wish to differentiate costs based on passage class to reflect the quality differences:

### Restocking Costs per Stateroom (per Maintenance Period)
- **High Passage:** Cr1500-2000 
  - Premium food and beverages
  - Luxury toiletries and amenities
  - Fresh linens and enhanced entertainment
  - Quality jump-sickness medications included
  
- **Middle Passage:** Cr1000 (standard)
  - Adequate meals with some variety
  - Basic toiletries
  - Standard linens
  - Basic medical supplies
  
- **Basic Passage:** Cr500-750
  - Minimal rations and water
  - No amenities beyond essentials
  - Communal supplies only
  - Passengers often bring their own comfort items

- **Low Passage:** Cr100 (as per standard rules)
  - Life support chemicals only
  - No food or amenities needed

This optional rule makes operating luxury passenger service more expensive but also explains the significant price differences between passage types. Ships can still choose to use standard supplies for all passengers and pocket the difference, but discriminating travelers may notice.

## Referee Notes

Jump-trauma provides excellent roleplaying opportunities:
- New characters making their first jump
- Veterans displaying coping mechanisms or quirks
- Plot hooks involving misjumps or extended jumps
- Character development through shared jump experiences
- Cultural flavor through different societies' approaches

The mechanical effects of jump sickness add meaningful consequences to travel choices. Players must weigh the comfort and reduced recovery time of expensive passages against the longer disorientation of budget travel. This creates interesting tactical decisions about when to arrive at a destination and how much to spend on passage.
