# The Star-Hopper Inn

**Referee's Note:** This is a modular encounter that can be dropped into any starport or downport. The Star-Hopper is a galaxy-wide franchise of budget accommodations, known for its predictable mediocrity. It serves as a place for the players to stay cheaply and, more importantly, as a potential source of information.

> **Read Aloud:** The sign for the "Star-Hopper Inn" is missing a few pixels, making it flicker erratically. The lobby is a sterile, soulless space that smells faintly of industrial cleaning chemicals and recycled air. The floor is made of a cheap, scuffed polymer, and the furniture is bolted to the floor. A row of flickering holographic advertisements on the wall cycles through ads for local shipping companies and questionable street food. The only sound is the low hum of the life support and the occasional, mournful chime of a malfunctioning autochef in the corner.

### The Staff

The Star-Hopper Inn is run by a skeleton crew of two, a clerk and a cleaner.

#### The Clerk: K'tarr

> **Read Aloud:** Behind the check-in counter, inside a booth of scratched plexiglass, sits a Gilan. It's a tall, thin alien with four long, delicate arms, each ending in three slender fingers. Its large, dark eyes blink slowly and independently of each other. It wears the crisp, clean uniform of the Star-Hopper franchise, and its movements are precise and economical. It is currently using all four hands to operate four different data terminals simultaneously without any sign of stress. It stops its work, focuses two of its eyes on you, and speaks in a perfectly modulated, monotone voice. "Welcome. Do you have a reservation?"

K'tarr is a stickler for the rules. The Star-Hopper corporate manual is its bible. It is not hostile, but it is utterly inflexible. It will not accept bribes, it will not respond to threats, and it will not bend a single regulation without a logically sound, officially sanctioned reason.

#### The Cleaner: Magda

> **Read Aloud:** A battered cleaning cart, laden with stained towels and chemical sprayers, is being pushed by a woman who looks as old and worn as the inn itself. Her grey hair is pulled back in a tight bun, and her face is a roadmap of wrinkles. She moves with a slow, practiced efficiency, her eyes missing nothing but revealing nothing. She doesn't look at the guests, and they don't look at her. She is, for all intents and purposes, invisible.

Magda has worked at this Star-Hopper for twenty years. She has seen everything and everyone. She is the true memory of the inn. She cleans the rooms, empties the trash, and overhears the secrets of the guests. She is deeply cynical and trusts no one, but she is also a font of information if the players can earn her trust by treating her like a human being rather than part of the furniture.

### The Room Experience

> **Read Aloud:** The hallway is a long, featureless tube. Your room is a small, featureless box. The bed is a thin mattress on a molded plastic frame. The 'fresher unit makes a strange gurgling sound every few minutes. The walls are paper-thin; you can hear the couple in the next room arguing about their shared credit chip. The "autochef" offers a single option: a lukewarm, nutrient-rich paste that tastes vaguely of chicken. It is not a room for rest; it is a room for waiting.

### Task Chain: Prying Information from the Staff

The players might need to get information about a current or former guest. They have two avenues, each requiring a different approach.

#### Option 1: The Official Route (K'tarr) (TN 9+)
K'tarr has access to the official guest registry, payment logs, and security camera footage. He will not share this without a very good reason.
*   **The Check:** This requires an **Advocate**, **Persuade (Deception)**, or **Computers** check.
    *   **Advocate:** Convince K'tarr that a specific corporate regulation *requires* him to share the information.
    *   **Persuade (Deception):** Impersonate a corporate official or law enforcement and convince him you have the authority to view the data.
    *   **Computers:** If all else fails, hack his terminal from a distance.
*   **Success (9+):** K'tarr, satisfied that he is following protocol (or unaware of the breach), provides the specific, sterile data requested: check-in/check-out times, payment method, etc.
*   **Failure (8-):** K'tarr quotes Regulation 47-B, subsection 9 ("Guest Privacy Mandates") and refuses to speak further on the matter.

#### Option 2: The Unofficial Route (Magda) (TN 8+)
Magda knows the things the cameras don't see: who met with whom, if a guest seemed nervous, what they threw away.
*   **The Check:** This requires a **Streetwise** or **Persuade** check. A simple bribe will fail; she's seen enough credits to know they're more trouble than they're worth.
    *   **Streetwise:** Approach her with the quiet respect of a fellow fringe-dweller who knows the score.
    *   **Persuade:** Treat her with genuine kindness. Offer her a hot cup of real coffee, ask about her day, and show that you see her as a person.
*   **Success (8+):** Magda stops her work for a moment and, without making eye contact, shares a piece of valuable, observational gossip. "The man in 304? He had a visitor last night. A Vargr with a scar over his left eye. They were arguing in whispers. He left in a hurry."
*   **Failure (7-):** Magda ignores the players completely, continuing her work as if they don't exist. She will not respond to further attempts.
